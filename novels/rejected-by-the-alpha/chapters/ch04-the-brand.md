# Chapter 4: The Brand

\[Wren's POV\]

Smoke curled up from her collarbone in a thin gray thread. Every breath Wren dragged in tasted like burnt hair and old pennies.

The mark had stopped spreading. Whatever shape it had been building beneath her skin, it was finished now: dark and raw and already cooling into scar.

She caught a glimpse of it when she finally looked down. Black lines knotted together just above her collarbone, ugly and permanent, nothing like the gold thread that had connected her to Kieran minutes ago.

The lines had gone fully black now, the last thread of smoke thinning away above them. They caught the light instead of reflecting it, swallowing every flicker that touched her skin.

Beneath the ruined skin, the second pulse in her chest kept beating anyway. Slower now, but still there, refusing to be sealed shut along with everything else.

Stay unremarkable, her mother used to say. Wren almost laughed.

She didn't have the air for it. There was nothing unremarkable left to become.

Two guards broke from the platform's edge before she got her knees back under her. Hands closed around both arms at once and hauled her upright.

"Up," one of them said, terse, already pulling.

Her palms were still scraped raw from the fall. She hadn't noticed until the guards' grip bit into her arms hard enough to eclipse it.

Her legs buckled the moment they let go of her weight. She braced for the pain to follow. It didn't come, only a strange, ringing quiet where the pain used to live.

The ringing was starting to thin, and underneath it she could hear how quiet the amphitheater had actually gone. Eight hundred wolves, and not one of them speaking above a whisper.

Yseult crossed to the platform's center, robes sweeping over the stone where Wren had fallen seconds earlier. Her voice reached everyone without effort.

She lifted one hand toward Wren without quite touching her, two fingers extended as the ritual required for a sealing. The gesture looked almost gentle. It wasn't.

"The bond is broken," she said. "The mark is sealed."

A ripple ran through the crowd, quieter than before. Nobody argued with a sealed mark. Nobody ever did.

"Let it be known," Yseult went on. "Wren Ashveil carries the Sanctum's brand."

"No pack owes her shelter. No hearth owes her a place."

"Any pack that crosses her path is bound by that same law to report the sighting, not offer quarter."

Somewhere in the lower lines, a mother's hand came up over a child's eyes. An old habit, Wren realized dimly.

People had seen this mark before. They'd just never seen it on someone they'd shared a border with.

Among the closest lines, a cluster of visiting Alphas watched with the flat, assessing look of men filing away a fact for later use. None of them looked away out of kindness.

"Worth remembering," one of them murmured to the wolf beside him. His eyes went straight to Wren's collarbone before he'd even finished saying it.

Every wolf in this amphitheater would carry that warning home tonight. Every wolf who heard it after would look at her collarbone before they looked at her face.

She found Kieran without meaning to. He hadn't moved from the platform's edge, the scroll still caught in his fist, more creased now than it had been a minute ago.

His jaw worked, mouth searching for something to say into the silence Yseult had just left behind. He almost said something. Whatever it was, it didn't survive past his teeth.

He took half a step toward the platform's center before catching himself and going still again.

His knuckles had gone white around the scroll again, same as they had before he'd spoken the rejection. Whatever he wanted to say, he was holding it down just as hard.

The guards started walking her toward the lower gate, one on each side. Wren's feet dragged against the stone until she made them cooperate. Begging hadn't slowed the bond's recoil, and it wouldn't slow this.

The crowd drew back as the guards walked her through the lines. Nobody wanted to be close enough to catch whatever she carried.

A few wolves spat toward the stone at her feet. Most just watched.

Somewhere near the back, a low howl started and died just as fast, cut off before it could gather other voices. Nobody wanted to be the one who kept it going.

Lira's eyes found hers across the platform, wide and wet, before a guard's shoulder blocked the view entirely.

Somewhere behind her, Lira's voice broke free of the crowd. "She's bleeding, someone look at her."

A guard's hand closed over Lira's arm the instant the words left her mouth, an overstep that would have cost him dearly on any other day. Nobody answered her either.

Wren stopped trying to make her own voice work and started noticing things instead. The grip on her left arm was looser than the one on her right. The lower gate ahead stood only half open.

Aldric rose from the royal box before the guards had taken her three full steps. He didn't hurry. Nothing here moved except on his schedule.

Kieran's head turned toward his father. For the first time since the rejection left his mouth, he looked ready to interrupt something he had no right to interrupt.

"By the Exile Law," Aldric said, "she is cast beyond the Rite's protection."

A pause stretched long enough that the crowd seemed to hold its breath with her.

"She crosses the Wilds' border before dawn."

No one argued. No one ever did, not once the King had spoken the law himself.

Not even Kieran. His mouth opened again and closed without a sound, the argument dying somewhere between his chest and his teeth.

"Garrick." Aldric's voice again, flat. "See that she reaches the gate."

For one moment, as the guards shifted their grip to hand her off, her path crossed close enough to the royal box that she caught Aldric's eyes.

There was no surprise in them. His shoulders had eased, just slightly, the tension gone out of them for the first time since the Rite began.

It was the same look he'd worn the moment the bond first caught fire between her and his son, the one that had never made sense until right now.

Garrick's eyes cut to Kieran first, some question in them that Kieran didn't answer. Then he stepped down onto the platform and took Wren's arm from the guard who'd been dragging her.

His fingers closed around her arm, careful and sure. Nothing like the guards' grip.

"Walk," Garrick said, low enough that only she could hear it. "Don't give them a reason to drag you."

He didn't meet her eyes when he said it.

No trial. No chance to speak. Just a king's voice, a beta's hand closing over her arm, and a sentence already being carried out before she'd had time to understand it had even been passed.
