# Chapter 10: A Name Half-Remembered

\[Wren's POV\]

Tamsin did not answer her own question, and she did not ask it again.

She finished the cleaning. She stitched the wound along Wren's ribs with a curved needle and a thread that smelled of pitch, and she did it well, and she did all of it without saying one word.

Wren counted the stitches instead of screaming. There were fourteen.

The silence was the worst part. Not the needle.

"You asked me something," Wren said, when she could.

"I did."

"You going to tell me why."

"No."

Tamsin bit the thread off close to the skin and sat back on her heels, and her face gave away nothing at all.

"Sleep," she said.

Wren slept.

She woke to gray light through a gap in the turf and the smell of something boiling, and for a moment she did not know where she was, and then all of it arrived at once and sat on her chest.

The kingdom had watched a man reject her. Her own pack had shut their doors. Four men had walked her nine miles to kill her, and a stranger had dragged her out of the snow and then gone silent over something on her own skin.

She lay there and let it finish landing.

Tamsin was at the fire with her back turned.

"I'm awake."

"I know."

"You've been waiting for me to be."

"I've been making broth." Tamsin lifted the pot off the hook without turning round. "You'll drink it and you won't like it."

She did not like it. She drank all of it.

Over the next stretch of hours, Wren learned the shape of the hut without moving from the pallet. One room. A fire, a pallet, a work table, a shelf of stoppered jars set in an order that was clearly deliberate and clearly not alphabetical.

Bundles of dried something hanging from the beams, enough of them that the ceiling had a second ceiling made of leaves.

And a door with three separate bars on it, which was two more than anyone needed against weather.

"You've been here a long time," Wren said.

"Nineteen years."

"On your own."

"Mostly."

Tamsin was grinding something in a bowl and did not look up while she did it. She had a way of answering that closed the subject without ever refusing it, and Wren, who had spent a lifetime being carefully unremarkable, recognized the technique perfectly.

She was being managed.

"You knew my name before I gave it."

"I said word crosses the border. It does."

"From who? You're nine miles past the last checkpoint and there's nobody out here."

The grinding stopped for half a beat, then carried on.

"There's more out here than nobody," Tamsin said. "You'll learn that soon enough. Rest."

Wren rested. She had no other options and she knew it.

But she watched.

She watched Tamsin change the dressing twice that day and both times find a reason to look at the skin above the wound, and both times go still for a fraction of a second before carrying on.

She watched Tamsin's eyes go to the door when the wind changed.

Once, late, she saw her cross the hut and stand over the pallet a long moment, thinking Wren asleep, and then go back to the fire without having done anything at all.

That was the one that frightened her.

Not because it was threatening. Because it wasn't. It was the standing of a woman trying to decide something about a person who cannot yet be asked.

Wren kept her breathing even and her eyes shut and thought, very clearly, that she had traded one room where people knew things about her for another.

The fever came up in the night.

She felt it start, which she had not expected. A crawling heat along the backs of her arms, and then the cold that follows it, and then the room going soft at the corners.

Tamsin's hand on her forehead. Tamsin swearing, quietly and without heat, in a way that suggested she had expected this and hoped to be wrong.

After that it stopped being a night and became a series of rooms.

In one of them she was on the platform again, and the light from the oculus had weight, and she could not get out from under it. In one of them her mother was pressing her shoulders and saying the two words. In one of them there was snow on her forearm that would not melt.

And in one there was a voice.

Not hers. It was using her mouth, but she was not the one choosing the words, and the sensation was so wrong that it nearly surfaced her.

She heard herself say something. A word she did not know. It came out whole and easy, with an old shape to it, the shape of a word that has been said a great many times by a great many people who are no longer alive to say it.

"Emberveil."

She heard it out loud and did not understand it.

Then she heard something else. A bowl going over. The ring of metal hitting the floor and rolling and coming to rest.

Hands came down on her shoulders and gripped hard enough to bruise.

"Say it again."

Wren was too far under. She turned her face away from the voice.

"Girl." The grip tightened.

"Say that again. Where did you hear that. *Who said that to you.*"

She surfaced just enough to get one eye open.

Tamsin was over her, and the firelight was on her face, and all the color had gone out of it. Her mouth was open. Her hands on Wren's shoulders had started to shake.

Nineteen years alone nine miles past the border, three bars on the door, and nothing had put that on the woman's face until now.

"It's a name," Wren said, from somewhere very far away. "Isn't it."

Tamsin did not answer.

She let go, and stood, and crossed to the door, and put her hand flat against the topmost bar. She stayed there with her back turned for a long time.

Then she began, very quietly, to bar it.
