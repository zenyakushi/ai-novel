# Chapter 15: Sealed Records

\[Kieran's POV\]

He went back the next morning and Oren would not look at him.

"I've told you what I can, Alpha."

"You told me more than you meant to."

"Then I'll be more careful today." The old man's hands stayed flat on the counter. "You should not have come back."

"Who instructed you. On the first."

"Alpha."

"A name, Oren. That's all I want and then I'll go."

Oren looked at him for a long moment, and there was something in his face that Kieran did not want to identify, because identifying it would mean admitting he had walked down here and put an old man in front of a thing that could cost him.

"I have a daughter in the mill row," Oren said.

That was the whole answer. Kieran heard all of it.

He put both hands on the counter and leaned on them and said nothing for a while, and his chest had gone tight in a way that had nothing to do with the air down here.

"I'm sorry," he said. "I shouldn't have come back."

"No, Alpha. You shouldn't."

He was at the bottom of the stairs when Oren spoke again, quietly, without raising his head from his work.

"The Spire keeps a duplicate. Everything the archive holds, they hold a copy. It's section four as well."

Kieran stopped.

"That doesn't help me. I can't walk into the Spire and ask."

"No," Oren agreed. "You can't."

And that was all he said, and Kieran went up the three flights understanding that he had just been told something and not at all sure what.

Lira found him on the north wall at dusk.

He heard her coming and did not turn around, and she came and stood beside him and put her elbows on the stone in the exact way their mother had always told her not to.

"You've been down in the archive twice."

"Word travels."

"Word ran." Lira's voice was light and her face was not.

"Garrick told me. Before you take that badly, he told me because he's frightened for you and he thought I could do something about it."

"Can you?"

"No."

They stood there. Below them the pack grounds were going dark, torches coming up one at a time along the processional path, and somebody down there was laughing at something.

"They repainted the shutters," Kieran said.

"What?"

"At the baker's corner. Two years they've needed it. It got done this week."

Lira did not answer for a moment.

"I noticed that too," she said.

He turned to look at her properly for the first time.

She was not looking at him. She had her eyes on the torches and her jaw set, and he had known his sister for twenty-four years and had never once seen her hold her face that carefully.

"Lira."

"Don't."

"What did you do."

"I want you to understand something first." Now she did look at him, and her voice had dropped.

"I'm not brave. I've never been brave. I have spent my whole life being the one who notices things and says nothing about them, and I have been very comfortable."

"Lira."

"And on the first of this month I was in the east hall to collect a seal for the household ledger, and I heard Father tell an archivist that a record would be collected and not entered." Her hands had gone white at the knuckles on the stone.

"Two days before the Rite. I heard it the same as you heard it after."

Kieran's breath went out of him.

"You've known for eleven days."

"I've known for eleven days that something was being arranged. I didn't know what." Her chin came up.

"Then I watched you say the words, and I watched them brand a girl in front of eight hundred people, and I have not slept properly since."

"Why didn't you come to me."

"Because you're his son," Lira said, "and until yesterday I had no way of knowing which of those two things you'd be."

That landed harder than anything Oren had said.

She reached into her sleeve.

"I copied it," she said.

"Copied what."

"The record. On the second, when it came in from the ceremony and before the courier came on the third." She had a folded square of paper in her hand and was not yet giving it to him.

"I had one night with it. I got perhaps a third."

Kieran stared at her.

"You went into the archive at night and copied a sealed Rite record."

"Yes."

"If Father..."

"I know what happens if Father." She was very pale. "Do you want it or not."

He took it.

It was a poor copy, done fast, the hand cramped and the lines running uphill. Ritual formulae he knew by heart. The list of names tested, in order, with their outcomes beside them.

Flicker. Flicker. None.

Flicker.

And near the bottom of what she had managed, a line that was not ritual language at all.

It was an annotation. Somebody had written it into the record in a different hand, the way a clerk adds a reference note, and Lira had copied it exactly, including the abbreviation.

*Ashveil, W. Cross-ref. Reg. Bloodline: EMBERVEIL. Flag standing, nineteen yrs. Disposition per standing instruction.*

Kieran read it three times.

"Emberveil," he said.

"That's what it says."

"It's not a pack." He looked up at her.

"Lira, I was raised on the lineages. Every ruling house, every minor line, every pack that's ever held a seat."

"I could recite them at nine years old. There is no Emberveil."

"I know. I looked."

"Where."

"Everywhere I can get to without asking anyone." Her voice was flat.

"The household lineages. The treaty rolls. The First Howl liturgy, the full version, not the one they read out."

"Nothing. Not one mention in anything I'm allowed to touch."

Kieran looked back down at the cramped, uphill writing.

*Flag standing, nineteen yrs.*

The girl had been nineteen years old. He had heard that somewhere in the days after and had not known what to do with it.

Somebody had put a mark against her name in a Sanctum register in the year she was born, and had kept it there her whole life, waiting for a ceremony that would bring her within reach.

And whatever they had marked her as belonged to a bloodline that, as far as the entire recorded history of the Alderwyn Dominion was concerned, had never existed at all.
