# Chapter 1: The Binding Moon

The branding iron sears into her skin before she understands what's happening.

Her knees buckle. Her palm slaps against the cold stone, and the smell of burning skin reaches her before the pain does.

She doesn't scream. She's spent nineteen years learning not to.

"Hold still," a voice says above her, flat and already bored. "It goes away faster if you hold still."

She can't see who's speaking. Her vision narrows to a single point of white heat crawling up her forearm.

Underneath it, something else answers. A second pulse, hers but not hers, hammering against her ribs like it wants out.

Then the heat, the noise, all of it, goes out at once. Black swallows the rest.

………………………………………………..………………..………………..………………..

"You're doing the thing again," the girl behind her said, tugging hard at the laces of Wren's ceremonial wrap.

"What thing?"

"The thing where you go somewhere else in your head." Another yank, harder than it needed to be. "Tonight of all nights, Wren."

Wren didn't answer. She watched her own hands in the cracked mirror propped against the wall, watched them stay steady when they shouldn't have.

Every other girl in the room was shaking. One had dropped her hairpin twice already. Another hadn't stopped talking since sundown, words tumbling out too fast.

"You're not even nervous," the girl said, and it wasn't a compliment.

"I'm nervous."

"You don't look it."

"I don't look a lot of things." Wren finally turned from the mirror. "Doesn't mean they're not there."

The girl's mouth twisted like she wanted to push further. Then the horn sounded outside, three low notes rolling across Duskmoor.

Every girl in the room went still at once. The Rite was starting.

Wren pressed a hand flat against her own ribs, under the borrowed ceremonial wrap. A second heartbeat had lived there for as long as she could remember.

Not a real one. She knew that. She'd checked, alone, at night, fingers pressed to her pulse point, counting.

One rhythm, always, no matter how sure she felt in the dark that there were two.

She'd never told anyone. Not the girl beside her. Not her mother, before she died.

Especially not the Sanctum registrars who came through the pack lands every few years with their ledgers and their soft, searching questions.

“Stay unremarkable”. Her mother's voice, the same two words every time.

Whatever you feel. Whatever you notice about yourself that the others don't have. Bury it.

Wren had spent her whole life being very good at burying things.

Outside, the pack grounds had transformed. Torches lined the processional path in long gold rows, smoke curling into a sky already stained violet with dusk.

Wolves from every rank packed the amphitheater tiers, betas and omegas shoulder to shoulder for one night where rank didn't decide where you sat. 

At the center, raised on a stone platform, Oracle Yseult waited, robes the color of old blood, hands folded like she was already tired of what was coming. Past her, at the head of the line reserved for House Draven, stood Kieran.

Wren had seen him before. Everyone had. He was the kind of Alpha you couldn't not see, tall enough that he never had to try.

Beside him, a broad-shouldered wolf leaned in and murmured something low. Kieran didn't answer. That had to be Garrick, his beta, standing close enough to matter and saying nothing anyone else was meant to hear.

A few steps back, a woman with the same sharp Draven cheekbones watched the crowd instead of the platform. Lira, his sister, looked like she'd rather be anywhere else.

Behind the three of them, seated apart on a raised bench of his own, King Aldric watched the platform with the stillness of a man who'd sat through this ceremony a hundred times before.

Tonight Kieran wore ceremonial black, a torque of hammered silver at his throat. An Alpha looked at a crowd to catalogue it, to price it. Kieran wasn't doing either.

He was looking at nothing. Or performing looking at nothing. Wren couldn't tell which, and not being able to tell needled at her.

Then his head turned, just slightly, and his eyes found hers across the whole packed amphitheater, like he'd already known where to look.

Her breath caught and didn't come back right away. She looked away first, heart slamming, and didn't let herself check whether he was still watching.

"Move," someone hissed behind her, elbowing her forward. "You're holding the line."

Wren moved.

The line gave her time to watch. Wolf after wolf stepped onto the moonlit sigil at the center of the platform, where the oculus above let a single column of light fall straight down.

Most nights ended there. A flicker across the skin, nothing more, no thread, no match, and the wolf stepped down and rejoined the crowd unremarked.

That was most of them. That was the expected outcome for a low-ranked girl from a nothing bloodline like Wren Ashveil.

She almost believed it, right up until Yseult called her name.

"Wren Ashveil."

Wren climbed the platform steps, and up close, Yseult's eyes were tired in a way that had nothing to do with the hour.

"Stand in the light," Yseult said.

Wren stepped onto the sigil. Moonlight poured down cold and clean, and for one held breath, nothing happened at all.

Then the second pulse, the one she'd spent nineteen years pretending didn't exist, slammed against her ribs so hard she nearly dropped to her knees.

Light bloomed under her skin, not the flicker she'd watched a hundred other wolves produce. A thread of gold-white light unspooled from her chest, bright enough that someone in the crowd gasped out loud.

It didn't fade. It stretched.

Straight toward the head of the Draven line. Straight toward Kieran.

He went rigid, his spine pulling him straight before he had any say in it. The same thread lit under his own skin, racing up from nowhere, and across the amphitheater, eight hundred wolves came up out of their seats at once.

"That's not..." someone near the front started, and didn't finish.

A voice cracked out her name from somewhere in the crowd, high and disbelieving. Wren couldn't turn to find who it belonged to.

Yseult's mouth flattened for one second before she caught it. Her face went smooth again. Anyone who looked a moment later would have found nothing there at all.

Kieran was staring at Wren now. Really staring, jaw slack, one hand rising halfway toward her before he caught himself and let it fall.

Behind him, a man in Sanctum gray leaned toward King Aldric's ear. Aldric's jaw went tight. He didn't look surprised.

He looked like a man who'd already known what was coming. Something about his stillness snagged harder than the noise around it, and Wren's eyes stayed on him a beat too long before the thread pulled her attention back to Kieran. 

The thread between them held. It didn't flicker, didn't fade.

Under her chest, the second pulse she'd buried for nineteen years finally had an answer.
