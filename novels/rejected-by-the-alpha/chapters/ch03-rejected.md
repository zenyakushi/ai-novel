# Chapter 3: Rejected

\[Wren's POV\]

The thread of light hadn't faded. It stretched from her chest to his, thin and burning gold, and eight hundred wolves stared at both ends of it like they were watching something forbidden.

The light from the oculus above her had weight to it, pressing down on her shoulders like a hand. She hadn't expected that. No one ever mentioned that part.

Her knees wanted to buckle from carrying it this long. She kept her spine straight anyway. Stay unremarkable, her mother used to say, and here she was, the whole amphitheater staring like she'd never be unremarkable again.

Too late for that now.

The murmurs had started somewhere behind her, low and fast, spreading through the lines like fire finding dry grass. Wren couldn't make out most of it, only fragments breaking loose from the noise: unnatural, never seen it hold like that, something's wrong. Every eye in the amphitheater was on the thread stretched between her chest and his.

She'd never had eight hundred wolves look at her at once. She hadn't known it would feel like drowning.

Kieran stood at the edge of the platform with the scroll open in both hands. His knuckles had gone bloodless around the parchment.

He hadn't spoken yet.

Behind him, King Aldric sat very still in the royal box. His hands stayed loose on the armrests. No lean forward, no breath caught like the rest of the box around him.

She filed that away without knowing why it mattered. There wasn't room in her chest right now for anything except the light still burning under her collarbone and Kieran staring at it like it might bite him.

The silence stretched long enough that Wren wondered if the ritual even had words for a bond this bright. Then Kieran's mouth opened.

"By the word of the Sanctum," he said. "And the law of the Rite."

His voice came out steady. Too steady, pressed flat and held there, and his knuckles on the scroll had gone white with the same effort.

Wren's fingers curled at her sides, nails biting half moons into her palms. She made herself watch his face instead of the parchment.

"I, Kieran Draven, Alpha of Duskmoor." A pause, his throat working. "Do not accept this bond as sealed."

The words landed like the ground had vanished beneath her feet. She felt them before she understood them, a cold drop through her stomach that had nothing to do with the light still humming under her collarbone.

He stopped.

Just for a second. But Wren saw it. His eyes came up off the scroll and found hers, and something moved behind them that wasn't duty.

One hand slid an inch down the scroll's edge, starting to lower it, starting toward her, before he caught himself and gripped the parchment tighter instead.

"Kieran." Yseult's voice, low, a warning dressed up as a prompt.

He looked back down at the parchment like it had burned him.

His mouth opened again and no sound came out the first time. Yseult's chin dipped, a fraction, fast enough that no one behind her could have caught it.

"I name this joining unfit." Each word dragged slower than the last out of his throat. "I reject the mate the bond has offered."

Somewhere in the tiers, a single voice broke free of the hush. "No." It was swallowed instantly by hundreds more, but Wren heard it.

Somewhere behind Kieran, Lira's hand came up over her own mouth. Garrick didn't move at all, and he didn't take his eyes off the back of Kieran's head.

The thread between them went white.

Not gold anymore. White, and searing, and Wren's whole chest seized around it like a fist closing tighter with every breath she tried to take.

She heard herself make a sound she didn't recognize.

"Please," she said, though she hadn't decided to say it, and the word barely made it past her teeth before the light answered instead of him.

The light didn't fade this time. It snapped.

It whipped back into her like a rope cut loose from the other end, faster than anything should move. The force of it threw her head back and buckled both knees at once.

She hit the platform hard enough to lose her breath.

Above her, the crowd's silence broke into noise, gasps and shifting bodies and someone shouting her name, the sound folding in on itself until she couldn't tell whose voice it was.

Her ears rang too loud to sort any of it out.

Beneath the pain, something else stirred. A second pulse, low in her chest, the one she'd spent her whole life pretending not to feel.

It had never moved like this before. It moved now, hard and fast, knocking at her ribs from the inside.

Wren pressed a hand flat over her sternum, gasping against the stone under her palms. Her vision kept sliding sideways, the torches smearing into long orange streaks, the taste of copper rising at the back of her throat.

She scanned what little of the crowd she could still see through the blur, some old reflex hunting for a face that might help. She didn't find one.

Through the ringing she caught Garrick's arm shoot toward Kieran again, the same steadying grab as before, bracing for an Alpha who might go down too.

Kieran didn't move. He stood frozen at the platform's edge, the scroll hanging loose from one hand, staring down at her like he'd forgotten the rest of the ritual existed.

"Kieran." Garrick's voice, tight and low.

"The rite isn't finished. You have to name the sentence."

For half a second, before Yseult stepped between them, Kieran's throat moved like he was swallowing something he couldn't say. His hand tightened on the scroll until the parchment creased.

Lira had started forward too, robes bunched in one fist, before a guard's arm blocked her path without a word. She didn't try again. She just stood there and watched, and didn't look away once.

Then heat bloomed at her collarbone, sharp enough to cut through everything else.

She looked down. Her skin was smoking, a thin acrid smell rising off it that made her stomach turn.

A mark was forming right where the bond's light had split open moments before. Dark lines spread under her skin, not pressed on from above but pulled up from somewhere beneath, like ink bleeding through wet paper from the other side.

It didn't look like a brand yet. It looked like it was still deciding what shape to take, and it was choosing that shape out of her.

Wren dug her nails into the stone and tried to crawl backward out of the light. Her arms wouldn't hold her weight, and the stone scraped raw against her palms.

The pain climbed from her collarbone up into her throat. For a moment she couldn't tell if the smoke was rising off her own skin or if her eyes had simply stopped working right.

Beneath the burn, the second pulse kept beating, faster now, throwing itself at the underside of the mark before it could finish closing over.

Kieran was still watching her. Even with the mark spreading under her skin in front of the whole kingdom, he hadn't looked away once.

She opened her mouth to scream. The heat swallowed the sound before it left her throat.
