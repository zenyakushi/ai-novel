# Chapter 2: Alpha of Duskmoor

\[Kieran's POV\]

"Say it again," Garrick said. "Faster. You sound like you're reciting names off a muster roll."

Kieran repeated the invocation under his breath, clipped and flat. Garrick shook his head anyway.

"Still too slow."

"It's a sacred rite, not a drill."

"Tell that to eight hundred wolves who'd rather be home by moonhigh."

A servant fastened the silver torque at his throat. The metal went cold against his skin before it warmed, and Kieran didn't flinch. He'd stood through this fitting every Rite season since he was seventeen.

Garrick studied him in the mirror instead of himself. "You look like you already know how tonight ends."

"It ends the way it always ends." Kieran rolled his shoulders under the weight of ceremonial black.

"Someone lights up, someone doesn't. I officiate. I go home."

"Optimistic."

"Realistic."

The door opened without a knock. King Aldric never knocked in his own hall, and every servant in the room went still, eyes down.

"Leave us," Aldric said.

The servants filed out first. Garrick caught Kieran's eye on his way past, something unreadable in it, before he followed them and pulled the door shut behind him.

Kieran straightened out of habit more than respect. "Father."

Aldric circled him once, slow and appraising, and did not trouble to hide that he was doing it. "Tonight matters more than most."

"Every Rite matters."

"Tonight isn't about you standing there looking like a portrait, Kieran." Aldric's eyes stayed flat, unreadable. "It's about necessary outcomes."

Kieran's jaw tightened. "Meaning?"

"Meaning you do exactly what the ritual asks of you. Nothing more."

"The ritual doesn't ask anything. It runs its course, and I stand there."

"Not always." Aldric's jaw flexed, just once. "Tonight it might ask more of you than usual."

"Ask what, exactly?"

"Whatever it asks. You'll do it anyway." Aldric straightened his own collar in the mirror behind Kieran, not meeting his eyes.

"You always do."

Kieran opened his mouth to press further. His father was already walking away, robes snapping behind him.

Something cold settled low in his stomach, and he couldn't name what it was.

He didn't take the main corridor. The servants' passage cut behind the west hall and came out closer to the amphitheater, and he'd been slipping through it since he was young enough to hate crowds.

He didn't expect anyone else to be using it tonight.

His father's voice reached him first, low and pitched for one listener, not for a hall full of servants.

"It must go as planned tonight, Yseult. No deviations. Not if something surfaces."

Kieran stopped dead behind the corner.

Yseult's answer came thinner, tighter. "And if the girl herself notices?"

"She won't know what she's looking at. None of them ever do."

Kieran stood very still, one hand flat against the wall. He didn't breathe again until the footsteps moved off in the other direction.

He told himself it was nothing. Sanctum business, above his rank, none of his concern.

Lira caught him at the base of the amphitheater steps, ceremonial robes already in place. "You've got your father's face on."

"I don't have a face."

"You have exactly one face, and it's the one you wear when you've decided not to ask a question." She fell into step beside him. "What did he say to you?"

"Nothing worth repeating."

"That's not a no."

Kieran didn't answer. Lira let it sit there instead of pushing, and didn't bring it up again.

He almost believed it was nothing by the time he took his place at the head of the Draven line.

………………………………………………..………………..

By the time the horns sounded, the knot in his stomach hadn't loosened. It had only gone quiet, tucked under duty.

Garrick took his place at Kieran's shoulder, same as always. Lira fell in a step behind them, close enough to hear without either of them having to turn.

Wolf after wolf climbed the platform steps and stood on the moonlit sigil, waiting in the column of light. Most stepped down unmarked, and the crowd's attention drifted a little further each time.

Kieran watched from the head of the line without really seeing any of them. His mind kept circling two words. Necessary outcomes.

Then Yseult called a name he didn't recognize. A slight girl in a ceremonial wrap climbed into the light, and something in his chest lurched sideways before she'd even stopped moving.

He didn't understand it yet. He just felt it, a hook beneath his sternum, yanking.

Light split open across her collarbone, gold-white and violent, nothing like the flicker he'd watched all night. It didn't fade. It should have faded.

He'd watched close to forty of these tests light up or fizzle since he was young enough to be dragged along by his father. He'd never seen anything move like this.

It stretched across the whole amphitheater, straight at him.

The same light lit under his own skin a heartbeat later, and his knees nearly buckled with it. Garrick's hand shot out and closed hard around his forearm, steadying him before he could stumble.

"Kieran." Garrick's voice had gone rough. "What is that."

He couldn't answer. Every wolf in the amphitheater was on their feet, and behind him, Lira's breath caught, sharp enough that he heard it over the noise.

Yseult's composure cracked open for exactly one second. Her folded hands came apart, one gripping the other hard, before she smoothed her face back into place.

Then she stepped forward, hand raised, and the whole amphitheater dropped into a silence that hurt worse than the noise had.

"Wait." Her voice carried, sharp enough to cut through eight hundred indrawn breaths.

"This match does not seal. Not yet."

The girl on the platform flinched like she'd been struck.

It struck him, even through the shock, that she'd waited. Long enough that every wolf in that amphitheater had already seen the thread hold, had already seen it reach him. No one would be able to say later that the bond hadn't been real.

"The Elders pulled her line this morning," Yseult said, and Kieran's stomach dropped straight through the floor of the amphitheater. "Her bloodline is flagged. Unfit."

Necessary outcomes. His father's voice, low and certain, hours old and suddenly deafening.

Kieran looked past Yseult to where his father sat. Aldric wasn't surprised. He wasn't even pretending to be.

"Alpha Draven." Yseult's voice pitched itself for the whole crowd to hear. "Come forward."

Kieran's legs moved before his mind caught up to them. Every step up the platform felt longer than the last, eight hundred wolves watching him cross the space between the Draven line and the light.

Yseult was already holding a scroll toward him when he reached her, parchment pale under torchlight. His hand closed around it before he'd decided to take it.

His thumb found the wax seal. It had already been broken, and pressed shut again, before he'd ever left his chambers tonight.

He made himself look at her then, just once. She wasn't crying, and she wasn't shaking. She was watching him the way you'd watch a cliff edge you hadn't decided whether to jump from.

He unrolled the parchment far enough to catch the first line, and his stomach turned over. Nothing on the page matched a single word he'd drilled with Garrick that morning.

Eight hundred wolves waited for him to speak. Kieran looked down at words he'd never rehearsed, never wanted, and had no idea if his voice would work when he needed it to.
