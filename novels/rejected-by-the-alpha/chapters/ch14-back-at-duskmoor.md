# Chapter 14: Back at Duskmoor

\[Kieran's POV\]

Duskmoor went back to normal in four days, and that was what Kieran could not get past.

The mill road reopened. The market ran. Somebody repainted the shutters on the house at the baker's corner, which had needed doing for two years and had apparently become urgent.

Nobody mentioned the Rite. Not once, not in his hearing, and the completeness of it was too neat to be accidental.

On the fifth morning he found himself standing at the amphitheater's lower gate with no memory of deciding to walk there.

The platform had been swept. Of course it had.

There was nothing on the stone at all. Something went through his chest anyway, low down, the same thing that had been waking him at the fourth bell for five nights running.

"They scrub it the same day," Garrick said behind him. "Every time. It's in the rota."

"How long have you been following me."

"Since the stables." Garrick came up level with him, not looking at the platform. "You've walked past this gate four times in two days."

"I've had reason to be in the lower court."

"You've had reason to be in the lower court four times."

Kieran did not answer that.

"Ask me," Garrick said.

"Ask you what."

"Whatever it is you've been not asking since Tuesday. You're making a poor job of carrying it."

Kieran looked at the platform a moment longer.

"The scroll," he said. "The one Yseult put in my hand."

"What about it."

"The seal was broken before I took it. Broken and pressed shut again." He turned.

"I felt it under my thumb. It had been opened that morning at the latest."

Garrick's face did not change, which told him everything.

"You knew."

"I suspected. There's a difference and I've been holding onto it." Garrick rubbed the back of his neck.

"What do you want to do."

"I want to read the record."

"Kieran."

"Every Rite is recorded. Every word of the ritual, every deviation, every name. It goes to the archive within three days and it stays there."

Kieran had started walking. "It's five days. It's there."

"And when your father asks why the Alpha of Duskmoor is reading his own ceremony back?"

"Then he can ask me."

Garrick caught his arm, briefly, and let go almost at once.

"Think about what you're doing," he said quietly. "You haven't asked your father a single question since the Rite. Not one."

"Neither has anyone else. Four days of nobody asking anything, and you're about to be the first."

"I know."

"Do you know why nobody's asked?"

"Because they're afraid of him."

"No." Garrick's voice stayed low.

"Because they think it's finished. The moment you go into that archive, everyone in this pack finds out you don't."

Something closed in Kieran's throat and he swallowed past it.

Then he kept walking.

The archive was under the east hall, three flights down, and it was warmer than the rest of the keep because of the lamps. There was a smell of oiled leather and old paper that he had always liked as a boy and had not thought about in fifteen years.

The archivist was a thin man named Oren who had been there longer than Kieran had been alive.

"Alpha." He rose, and did not look surprised, and Kieran noticed that and set it aside.

"The Rite record. This season's."

"Certainly." Oren did not move toward the shelves. "May I ask the purpose?"

"No."

"Of course." A small nod. "It isn't available."

"It's five days."

"It is."

"The rule is three days to file."

"The rule is three days to file," Oren agreed. "It was filed on the second."

Kieran put his hand flat on the counter.

"Then bring it."

"I can't, Alpha. It isn't here." Oren's hands were folded and perfectly still.

"It was collected on the third."

The lamps made their small noise. Somewhere above them a door closed.

"Collected by whom."

"The Sanctum holds authority over ritual records. Section four."

"I didn't ask about section four. I asked who came down those stairs and took it."

Oren was quiet for a moment.

"A courier, Alpha. I did not know him."

"Was he Sanctum?"

"He carried Sanctum authority."

"That isn't what I asked either."

Oren said nothing at all, and the nothing went on long enough to become an answer.

Kieran made himself take his hand off the counter.

"When a record is collected," he said, "you enter it in the ledger. Date, item, who took it. Show me the ledger."

Something crossed the old man's face. It was gone at once, and what was left underneath it was not fear exactly. It was closer to apology.

"The ledger is a record of the archive's holdings," Oren said carefully. "It reflects what is held."

"Show me."

Oren brought it. He set it on the counter and opened it himself to the right page, and turned it around, and stepped back.

Kieran read the page twice.

There was no entry for the third. There was no entry for the second either. The page ran from the last day of the previous month to the sixth of this one, in the same steady hand, with no gap in the spacing and no room left for anything to have been removed.

It had not been altered. It had been written this way.

"You didn't record it," Kieran said.

"I was instructed that there was nothing to record."

"By the courier?"

"No, Alpha." Oren's voice had gone very quiet. "Before the courier."

Kieran raised his head.

"Before."

"I was told on the first," Oren said, "that this season's Rite record would be collected, and that the collection was not to be entered. I was told that two days before the Rite took place."

The lamps went on making their small noise. Kieran heard his own blood in his ears, too loud for a room with two men standing quietly in it.

He looked down at the ledger, at the unbroken run of dates in a careful hand, at the absence sitting in it where nothing at all was showing.

Someone had known there would be a record worth taking before there was a ceremony to make one.
