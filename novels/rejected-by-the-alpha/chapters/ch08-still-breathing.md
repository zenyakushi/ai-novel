# Chapter 8: Still Breathing

\[Wren's POV\]

The cold stopped being cold some time after the boots went away.

That frightened her more than the pain had, in the part of her still capable of being frightened. She had grown up on the same warnings every pack child got.

When you stop feeling it, you are not warmer. You are further along.

She tried to move her right hand and it did not report back.

Her left did. It came up a few inches out of the snow and stopped there, and she looked at it without much interest. It did not seem to have anything to do with her.

There was blood on the knuckles. Not hers, she thought. She could not remember earning it.

Snow had built along the length of her forearm while she lay there. It was not melting.

She had watched snow melt on her own skin her whole life without once thinking about it. It was the sort of thing a body did without being asked, and hers had stopped asking.

She understood what that meant and set the understanding down somewhere she would not have to keep looking at it.

Above her the sky had gone the flat gray that comes before light rather than after it. She had been here long enough for that to change. She had not noticed it changing.

Time was coming apart into pieces that did not touch each other.

A piece where she was cold. A piece where her mouth was full of iron. A piece where she was nine years old and her mother's hands were on both her shoulders, and the grip was too hard, and the words were the same two words.

Stay unremarkable.

Wren lay in the snow with her ribs open and thought, distantly, that she had done what she was told and it had not helped at all.

The next piece had her turned onto her side, which she did not remember doing.

The wound along her ribs had stopped its steady warm spreading, which should have been good news. She did not think it was good news. Things stopped when there was nothing left to come out of them.

She could hear her own pulse in her ears, thin and far off, slower than it should be.

And underneath it, the other one.

It had not slowed.

That was the thing she kept arriving back at, each time the pieces let her think at all. Everything in her was shutting down in the order the old wolves had always promised it would, and the second pulse had not changed its pace by a single beat.

It went on under her breastbone, steady and unhurried, like something keeping time in an empty room.

She pressed her working hand flat against her chest, over the brand, over the ruin below it.

*What are you.*

It did not answer. It had never answered. Nineteen years of pressing her fingers to her wrist in the dark and counting one rhythm, and it had never once let her catch it.

But it was here now, and the rest of her was going, and it was not going with her.

For the first time in her life she did not want it gone.

*Whatever you are. Stay.*

The snow came down on her face and she let it.

Some indeterminate while later she surfaced again, and the light had changed once more, and she was still breathing, and she could not account for why.

She had been bleeding for hours. She had counted the seconds at the start and lost the count somewhere and never found it again.

By every rule she knew, she should have finished a long time ago.

Her fingers had gone past numb into something else, a deep useless ache down in the bone. She flexed them anyway. They moved.

That was wrong too.

She thought about the enforcer's face when the command had failed. It had opened up for a second before he got it shut again, and whatever was under there had not been anger.

It had been the look of a man watching a door he had locked swing open on its own.

*He was afraid of me.*

The thought arrived whole and did not fit anywhere. She turned it over and could not make it sit with anything else she knew about herself.

A branded girl in a shift, nine miles past a border, bleeding out in the dark, and a grown enforcer with the Sanctum's authority behind him had looked at her for one second like she was the dangerous one.

Something in her chest, not the heart, took that in and kept its rhythm.

She did not sleep. She would have called it sleep if she had been anywhere else, but this had no edges to it, and no dreams, and she came out of it the same way you come up out of deep water, all at once and gasping.

What brought her up was sound.

Not wind. The wind had been going the whole time and she had stopped registering it hours ago.

This had weight behind it. Something taking the snow in steps, unhurried, coming down the slope she had failed to climb.

Wren went absolutely still, which was not difficult.

Four sets of boots had gone up that slope. She counted the steps coming down now and made it one.

One was worse. One meant someone sent, someone checking, someone who would not need to hurry because she had already done most of their work for them.

The steps stopped. Close enough that she heard the snow settle after them.

She could not lift her head to look. She lay with her cheek in the snow and her eyes open and the second pulse going steady under everything, and waited to find out what had come for her.

A long breath went out above her, slow, the sound a person makes when they have found something they were not looking for.

Then the steps came closer.
